Teach the expression loop

One body, different faces. The goal is to explain how a program orders and times the frames, not to draw a brand-new character.

Stage timer

05:00

  1. 00–04 · Mission and learning goals. Play the model; name what students will remember, practise and explain.
  2. 04–09 · Do Now. Project the starter. Give 20 seconds to think, then ask pairs to point to a pixel detail and propose a worried expression. Pupils answer four short checks independently.
    What to listen for and quiz key

    Surprised has a more open mouth. For worried, pupils might change the eyes or give it a downturned mouth, while keeping the body and leaf in place. Accept any idea supported by a visible detail. Quiz key: 1 A (layer), 2 C (three frames), 3 B (sheet PNG), 4 C (three costumes on one sprite).

  3. 09–12 · Types of Learning. Project the starting strategies. Have pupils look at their own Do Now answer and choose whether to revisit, watch a demonstration, or practise independently.
  4. 12–19 · Archive, frame model and Main Task 1A. Two reading checks, then the worked frame example and the guided Garden Guide animation. Ask what to fix if the body jumps. The sample stays in 1A.
  5. 19–31 · Main Task 1B. Pupils draw their own sprite or import one they made in theory. Make three distinct expressions and preview a full cycle.
  6. 31–35 · Loop model. Project the timing demo. Get a prediction and play both speeds before choosing a route.
  7. 35–51 · Main Task 2. Project the annotated Scratch route or browser Python route. Students test a change. A device problem can be recorded as a supported attempt, not a verified test.
  8. 51–54 · Learning Pit Stop. Project the two-step Pit Stop. Have pupils compare their runs or describe the next test they need. Only then ask them to choose a phase and explain why.
  9. 54–56 · Go Further / finish core. Fast finishers hold one expression longer. Help others finish the core animation.
  10. 56–60 · Plenary and submission. Collect the evidence PDF (or text note), native sheet and .sb3 or .py. The review can be completed as follow-up.

What to look for

  • Frame count: at least three distinguishable expressions, one aligned character.
  • Technical model: one sheet and individual frames; native dimensions are (16 × frame count) by 16.
  • Programming: deterministic start and visible loop, with a deliberate delay.
  • Reasoning: one observation comparing before and after a timing/order change.
