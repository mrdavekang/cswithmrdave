Stage timer

05:00

  1. 00–04 · Mission and learning goals. Play the model; name what students will remember, practise and explain.
  2. 04–07 · Do Now. Students identify which part changes when only the eyes change.
  3. 07–10 · Types of Learning. Use the three responses to decide which idea to model again.
  4. 10–18 · Archive, frame model and practice. Two reading checks, then the worked frame example and the practice board. Ask what to fix if the body jumps.
  5. 18–30 · Main Task 1. Make three distinct expressions and preview one full cycle. Students without yesterday’s work use Garden Guide.
  6. 30–34 · Loop model. Project the timing demo. Get a prediction and play both speeds before choosing a route.
  7. 34–50 · Main Task 2. Project the annotated Scratch route or browser Python route. Students test a change. A device problem can be recorded as a supported attempt, not a verified test.
  8. 50–53 · Learning Pit Stop. Review the evidence, then ask students to choose a phase and explain why.
  9. 53–56 · Go Further / finish core. Fast finishers hold one expression longer. Help others finish the core animation.
  10. 56–60 · Plenary and submission. Collect native sheet, evidence note and .sb3 or .py. The review can be completed as follow-up.

What to look for

  • Frame count: at least three distinguishable expressions, one aligned character.
  • Technical model: one sheet and individual frames; native dimensions are (16 × frame count) by 16.
  • Programming: deterministic start and visible loop, with a deliberate delay.
  • Reasoning: one observation comparing before and after a timing/order change.
