# Source map and teaching notes

**Version 3.5**, pp.28-30. The task follows the supplied weekly curriculum's
Term 1 Week 4 Sessions 2 and 3, combined into 60 minutes.

### Specific marking points retained

- June 2023 Q06: maximum 3 per threat area, maximum 4 overall. Do not impose
  an invented 2+2 allocation. Expanded statements about incorrect permissions,
  exposed/changed data, exploitable flaws and attack risks matter; vague claims
  such as "outdated software is more vulnerable" alone are not enough.
- June 2024 Q10.1: any 3 of attachment to another file/program, execution when
  the host opens/runs, replication/spreading, and harm when executed.
- June 2024 Q10.2: Trojan, spyware, adware, worm, ransomware and rootkit are
  accepted by the original scheme. The last three are accepted-answer notes,
  not additional core learning requirements in this lesson.
- June 2023 Q05.2: measures must be at business level. "Verify who sent an
  email" or "use a firewall" alone is not enough in that scheme.

## Textbook questions

- Printed p.241, Test yourself Q3: access level for students viewing results.
  Re-presented as a multiple-choice question. The follow-up about why editing
  results is inappropriate is explicitly teacher-created.
- Printed p.243, Test yourself Q1-4: short definitions of the four forms of
  social engineering. The recognition cases are teacher-created scaffolds.
- Printed p.245, Test yourself Q3 and Q4: spyware obtaining a password, and
  three possible effects of malware. Feedback is condensed from the supplied
  textbook's descriptions rather than labelled as an official exam scheme.

## Source distinctions and limits

The supplied specification defines pharming as redirection of website traffic
to another fake site. The textbook focuses on a fake site and discusses links
with phishing. The app explicitly states this distinction, and uses the
specification/June 2023 mark-scheme language for the assessed answer.

The textbook distinguishes unwanted advertising introduced as malware from
advertisements that the user agreed to see. That distinction is preserved.

Penetration testing belongs to specification 3.6.1 but remains in Week 5 of the
weekly curriculum. This lesson does not claim to complete the whole subsection.
There is no added content from general cyber-security websites or other boards.

## Formative use

Every written mark is human-recorded. The learner can choose self, peer or
teacher check, with optional checker initials. The app preserves the original
attempt before opening feedback. Improvements remain separate. Checking a
quiz is not evidence of an automatically awarded exam mark.

The November 2024 questions were present in the Session 1 reference resources.
They are deliberately retrieval questions, not an unseen diagnostic baseline.
