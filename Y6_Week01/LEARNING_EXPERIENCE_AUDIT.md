# Whole-site learning-experience audit

This audit reviews the pupil experience after the second instructional revision. It uses the same criteria on every screen so that attractive presentation does not conceal weak teaching.

## Instruction criteria

Every activity card should meet these eight checks:

1. **Context** — the pupil understands the situation or problem.
2. **Purpose** — the pupil knows why the action or concept matters.
3. **Preparation** — vocabulary or a model appears before assessment.
4. **One visible task** — the main instruction begins with a clear action and avoids unrelated demands.
5. **Response match** — the interface captures the response the instruction requests.
6. **Useful feedback** — success explains the reasoning; an error prompts a productive re-check.
7. **Manageable reading** — sentences are short, natural and visually chunked.
8. **Appropriate challenge** — the task moves beyond clicking toward applying, explaining, debugging or designing.

All main card instructions are 16–26 words. Longer explanations are split into labelled definitions, scenario panels, numbered steps or checklists.

## Layout and attention review

- The starter scenario is presented as one visual overview followed by three short narrative panels; the enlargement instruction is no longer mixed into the learning caption.
- On laptop screens, longer reference-and-response cards use two purposeful columns: the model or visual remains on the left while the pupil acts on the right.
- Those columns collapse to one at 700 px, with no horizontal overflow at 640 px or 768 px.
- The previously tallest compulsory cards were reduced substantially: the filename task from about 1515 px to 1004 px, build task from 1329 px to 840 px, testing task from 1479 px to 942 px, and modification task from 1740 px to 1048 px in the tested desktop view.
- Previous/Next still positions the next activity card below the fixed header rather than returning to the top of the website.

## Multilingual and accessibility evaluation

- Every core and extension instruction now has a matching Bahasa Melayu and Simplified Chinese support sentence.
- Read-aloud speaks the translated instruction with the selected language voice; it no longer reads English using a Malay or Chinese voice.
- Translations support task access while English Computing vocabulary remains visible for curriculum and Scratch transfer.
- Visuals have alternative text and captions, and essential relationships are repeated in ordinary text.
- MCQs, sentence frames and word banks reduce unnecessary typing without lowering the conceptual goal.
- Independent mode removes word-bank prompts while preserving the same assessment questions.
- Screenshot capture includes Windows, Chromebook and Mac guidance, a privacy warning and a teacher-check alternative.

## Remaining limitations for the teacher to monitor

- Browser speech voices vary across managed devices; test the three selected languages before the lesson.
- The app cannot inspect the live Scratch project. Build/test checklists and screenshot evidence therefore rely on honest pupil reporting and teacher circulation.
- Static translations should be checked against the school’s preferred Computing terminology.
- Pupils unfamiliar with switching tabs or moving a downloaded `.sb3` file may need a 30-second teacher demonstration.
- PDF creation opens the browser’s native print dialogue; wording and controls vary by operating system.

## Overall judgement

The revised site now provides a coherent KS2 learning experience rather than a collection of activities. The compulsory route teaches before it checks, moves from concrete routines to causal programming explanations, and makes pupils apply rather than merely recognise. The extension route adds genuine depth because its final levels require diagnosis and algorithm validation, not longer typing alone.

Recommended teacher judgement after device testing: **strong and teachable**, with live circulation still required during folder creation, Scratch construction and evidence capture.
