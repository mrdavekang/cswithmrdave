# Year 9 — App Playground: Your App, Your Choice

A 60-minute independent creative lesson with exactly two Main Tasks. The student route presents one teaching idea or question per slide, with visible progress within each activity. Six KSU starting checks have separate slides.

Public student link: https://mrdavekang.github.io/cswithmrdave/year9-app-design-theory/

Teacher preview: append `?teacher=1`. This unlocks navigation for rehearsal and shows guidance. It does not grant access to pupil data or to MIT accounts.

## Flow

| Stage | Minutes | What students do |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Read first | 4 | Meet the homepage design goal; tap two example controls |
| Do now | 3 | Answer three short checks, one per slide |
| Types of Learning | 3 | Identify prior knowledge and practice needs, including not tried yet |
| Main Task 1 — Explore | 11 | Browse six visible illustrated mock-ups; try controls; notice clear labels; choose any appropriate topic |
| Main Task 2 — Create | 22 | Name the app; select one feature and audience; choose logo and colours; sketch a homepage on paper or digitally; plan two taps; preview them |
| Start in MIT | 9 | Follow school-email setup; create and save a named project based on the student's own app |
| Further challenge | 3 | Begin a first MIT screen OR design a second screen with a route home |
| Learning Pit Stop | 2 | Select separate K/S/U confidence phases; see the majority or a tie |
| Showcase and save | 3 | Keep the idea, homepage and two planned taps in a PDF for Teams |

The overall aim is ideation and a first design. Finished code, live weather, purchases, bookings and an improvement paragraph are not required. The optional extra-time MIT challenge starts the appearance of the first screen; Blocks programming follows in later practical work.

## Design and access

The six illustrated mock-ups are original fictional examples: fashion, gaming accessories, cameras, weather, event bookings, and a timetable/timer. Interactive demonstrations use sample responses only. They are visible on the idea wall. Students can choose an unrelated topic, audience and layout.

The sketch editor supports movable text, picture placeholders or uploaded pictures, buttons, menus and inputs. Students can change widths, labels, logo and colours. Keyboard arrows, touch dragging and undo are supported. The equally prominent paper route directs students to collect paper from the front or ask their teacher; adding a photo is useful but not required. iPad hardware use should still be checked before class.

Tap previews simulate student-authored intentions; they are not generated MIT code. Each tap has a separate slide with the student's own sketch, a named control and a short response. No long planning paragraphs are needed. Mandarin and extra prompts are available throughout.

Forward navigation is sequential. Previous unlocked slides are available. MIT follows the completed design, rather than interrupting ideation. An explicit access-help route lets waiting students continue designing a second screen. Waiting for an account is not an attainment score. Existing-account users skip the new-email steps.

## Storage and reports

Responses remain in this browser. Save the PDF and editable JSON backup before switching devices. The report labels unattempted checks and missing work. It includes a second-screen sketch when created. The app does not upload to Teams automatically. No MIT password or email code is collected here.

Version 1 local work and backups migrate to the new format. Earlier ideas, sketches, responses and notes are retained. The new route restarts at the opening slide; a recovery copy of the previous local notebook is retained on-device. Archived visual-guide files remain to preserve old resource links; current teaching instructions are in the new slides.

## Visible simulation feedback

Taps now show the response on the example phone and in the event–response panel:

- Fashion saving: a brief heart pop and floating hearts settle into a saved state. Tap again to remove the favourite.
- Camera favourites: a star appears or is removed.
- Browsing and details: example cards are revealed; gaming comparison places two controllers side by side.
- Weather: sample city choices change the visible temperature; rain is a brief cue only for a rainy sample forecast. These are not live readings.
- Booking: choosing a sample time highlights it and shows a check. No booking is made.
- Planner: a real ten-second demonstration countdown supports pause, resume, reset and restart; the ring follows the remaining time. It pauses when leaving the slide, changing example or hiding the page.
- Student work: newly added sketch elements receive a short placement cue, and planned-tap previews use an icon matching the chosen effect.

Motion starts from an action and is finite. Reading, navigation, KSU checks and Pit Stop remain visually stable. Reduced-motion preferences disable movement and particles while preserving the visible saved state, selections and text feedback. No audio, new external requests or extra student questions were added.

## Answer-choice order

The three starter MCQs and the two-choice label check shuffle their answer choices for each new notebook. The order stays fixed on revisit, reload, language change and backup restore. Existing answers retain their meaning because marking and reports use the original answer ID, independently of the displayed position. English and Mandarin remain paired. Confidence scales and other ordered learning controls keep their original order.

## Clear starting-point checks

Each of the six Types of Learning slides opens with the specific “I can…” statement in a large learning card, followed by three plain-language choices: “I understand this”, “This is new to me”, and “I need help understanding this”. Mandarin uses the same meanings. New learning and requests for help contribute to the K/S/U focus counts; neither is an incorrect answer. Missing choices remain “Not selected”. Earlier “Not tried yet” responses are retained separately until the student chooses again. Main reading instructions and question text use larger type; learning choices stack when the available width is narrower.
