02

Main Activity 1 · 17 minutes

Classify without guessing

AO1 → AO2
Read before classifyingOxfordAQA textbook · PDF pp. 232–234 · Specification 3.5

Two independent decisions

Network scale: a LAN usually covers a relatively small geographical area and is commonly owned or managed by one person or organisation. A WAN spans a wider geographical area, often connects multiple sites and may have distributed ownership. The Internet is the largest WAN.

Connection method: wired connections transmit through physical cable. Wireless connections transmit using radio signals. A LAN may be wired, wireless or a mixture, so “wireless” does not mean “WAN”.

Wireless access supports mobility and avoids installing a cable for each device. Possible limitations include interference, obstruction, limited coverage and a greater opportunity for unauthorised interception if the network is not secured.

LANSmall area; often one site and one organisationWANWide area; often multiple sites and distributed ownershipTransmission methodWired cable, wireless radio or a mixture

03

Main Activity 2 · 25 minutes

Network architect challenge

AO2 · Justify
Technical briefingUse the properties—do not memorise a universal winner

Copper twisted pair carries electrical signals. It is comparatively inexpensive and suitable for many shorter wired connections, but electrical interference can affect its signal.

Fibre optic carries pulses of light. It offers higher bandwidth, supports longer-distance links and is resistant to electrical interference, although installation may be more expensive or specialised.

Wireless is appropriate when mobility or avoiding cabling matters. A valid recommendation must still recognise coverage, interference, reliability and security trade-offs.

Design briefExpanded school network
  • Twenty fixed administration desktops are each less than 40 metres from the network cabinet. Connections must be consistent and affordable.
  • A media building is 350 metres from the main server room. Students transfer large video files, and the route passes near electrical equipment.
  • Students carry tablets throughout the library and collaborative-learning spaces.
  • The school is opening another campus in Penang. Staff at both campuses need access to shared information.
Decision rule: identify the requirement, select an option, explain the relevant technical property, then state the consequence in this school.
1Administration desktopsShort · fixed · affordable
2Server room to media buildingLonger · large files · electrical equipment
3Student tabletsMobile · changing locations
4Kuala Lumpur campus to Penang campusSeparate geographical sites
5Recommend the complete arrangement for the expanded school.Balanced response

Refer to LAN and WAN, wired and wireless connections, copper and fibre, plus at least one limitation or trade-off.

Architect’s quality check

  • Every choice quotes or paraphrases a requirement from the brief.
  • Every explanation links a technical property to a school consequence.
  • You recognise that the same LAN can use copper, fibre and wireless.
  • You classify the second campus as a WAN without inventing its physical infrastructure.
  • You acknowledge at least one cost, coverage, interference, security or installation trade-off.
Teacher guidance · Main Activity 2

Likely choices: administration LAN/wired/copper; media link LAN/wired/fibre; tablets LAN/wireless; second campus WAN with physical medium not stated. Accept alternatives only when technically coherent and contextualised.
