    TYPES OF LEARNING

    What do I need to practise?

    5–7 min

    1 of 3 · Knowledge

    Try a small task, use help if needed, then identify your next step. This is not a grade.

    Knowledge: can I explain the key words?

    Can you explain both algorithm and decomposition?

    Find out here — then cover and recall

    Algorithm: a precise, ordered set of instructions for solving a problem. A Smart Badge algorithm tells it what to display and when.

    Decomposition: breaking a bigger problem into smaller parts. For our badge, plan the welcome display, button input and response separately.

    Flowchart key: an oval marks START/END; a parallelogram marks input/output; a rectangle marks a processing action; an arrow shows the direction to follow. Our simple badge model needs no process rectangle.

    Read the meanings. Cover them briefly and try explaining one to your partner. You will practise using these ideas in Main Task 1.

    Skills: can I make an instruction precise?

    The badge must display your initials when Button A is pressed. A trainee wrote: “Show something.” Rewrite this so someone knows exactly what should happen.

    Include: When does it happen? What exactly is displayed? Where does it appear?

    Sentence frame: When ___ is pressed, display ___ on the ___.

    Useful words: Button A · my initials · LED matrix. Choose the words that match the badge brief. If you are still stuck, leave the box for now and record that you need support.

    Understanding: can I read what the plan means?

    Find Button A in the flowchart. Follow its arrow to DISPLAY initials.

    What does that part of the flowchart mean?

    Point to the Button A input. Follow the arrow forwards: the next instruction is to display initials. The micro:bit processes the button event by following its stored instructions. The initials on the LEDs are the output. The arrow shows the sequence; it is not a wire or decoration.

    Trace this part again with your finger, then explain it to your partner or teacher.

    You may need to work on more than one area. These are next steps, not labels about what kind of learner you are.

    YOUR A4 PAPER TASK

    Create a flowchart for the same Smart Badge: it displays a welcome icon, waits for Button A, then displays your initials.

    Use my practice targets while drawing

    A model to adapt

    This badge welcomes a visitor with a heart. After Button A is pressed, it scrolls JP.

    Keep the structure, make the design yours. Choose your own welcome icon and initials. Do not simply copy the heart and JP.

    1. Choose, then draw on A4

    A small heart ♥, smile ☺ or diamond ◆ makes a clear welcome. These are LED patterns, not colour emojis. Use your initials after Button A (for example, AK or MH). Keep text short.

    1. Write Smart Badge Flowchart, your name and class at the top of your A4 paper. Sketch your chosen icon in the margin.
    2. Draw five large symbols from top to bottom, using the model beside you. Replace its heart and JP with your choices.
    3. Write an exact action inside each symbol: DISPLAY [your icon], WAIT until Button A is pressed, and DISPLAY initials “[your initials]”.
    4. Join the symbols with arrowheads. Trace the route with your finger: Does the badge wait before showing your initials?

    Shape reminder: oval = start/end; slanted parallelogram = input/output; arrow = order. No decision diamond is needed for this sequence.

    2. Explain your own drawing

    Write 2–4 short sentences. Say what appears first, what input it waits for, what appears next, and how the arrows show the order. Your teacher will read this; it is not a keyword test.

    Sentence starters
    First, my badge displays ___.
    It waits until ___. Then it displays ___.
    The arrows show ___. I chose ___ because ___.

    English example — adapt it: First, my badge displays a heart to welcome visitors. It waits until Button A is pressed, then scrolls JP. The arrows show the order from the welcome image to the initials.

    Plan in my own language first (optional)

    Write your ideas in Mandarin, Korean, Bahasa Melayu or another language. Use the English frame to try your own English version below. Ask a partner or your teacher to help translate; the app does not translate your writing automatically.

    You may use your own language here if you still need English support. Explain your design, not the example.

    3. Show your paper to your teacher

    Waiting? Save your explanation and continue. Your report will say teacher review pending, not “checked”. Keep your A4 paper for your teacher.

    Add a photograph or screenshot (optional)

    You do not need a camera to finish. If you want to attach an image, choose a file or paste it below. It stays in this browser and is included in your report.

    OPTIONAL EXTENSION

    Keep extending the same badge

    Early finishers

    Give visitors one more way to use your Smart Badge. Keep your welcome icon and Button A initials; design a different, useful response for Button B. Work on paper first. No device or photograph is required.

    LEVEL 1 · CHOOSE · ABOUT 3 MIN

    What should Button B do?

    An event is something the program responds to. Here, pressing and releasing Button B is the event; displaying your chosen image or text is the response.

    Example: “When Button B is pressed, display a smile on the LED matrix to welcome someone.” Borrow the pattern; choose a response with your own purpose.

    • Say hello: scroll “HI”.
    • Show readiness: display a tick.
    • Show a direction: display a left arrow to a welcome desk.
    • Share an interest: design a tiny music-note LED pattern.

    Or invent your own. Sketch a simple 5×5 pattern or use a short word. Avoid a full-colour emoji: the micro:bit uses LEDs.

    Language support

    LEVEL 2 · DRAW · ABOUT 5 MIN

    Two buttons, two response plans

    Your first flowchart showed one sequence. This version adds two separate event responses: each button triggers its own instructions after the welcome display.

    Use the back of your A4 paper or a fresh sheet. Write “On startup: display my welcome icon” across the top. Draw two columns underneath: Button A on the left and Button B on the right.

    1. Draw an oval START at the top of each column.
    2. Below it, draw a parallelogram INPUT: “Button A pressed” on the left; “Button B pressed” on the right.
    3. Draw a parallelogram OUTPUT: your own initials for A; the exact icon or text you chose for B. Sketch B’s output beside its symbol.
    4. Finish each column with an oval END response. Connect the symbols within each column with downward arrows.

    Keep the columns separate. Do not join A’s end to B’s start. After startup, either button can be used first. END finishes that response, not the whole badge. No decision diamond or calculation rectangle is needed for these simple responses.

    Language support

    Show the paper to your teacher when they are available. A photo is not required.

    LEVEL 3 · EXPLAIN & TEST · ABOUT 4 MIN

    Can your partner follow both responses?

    Use your finger to act as the computer; you do not need a working micro:bit. Ask a partner to choose a button, then follow only that column.

    1. Start the badge: point to your welcome icon.
    2. Press B first: which exact output should appear? Explain why pressing A beforehand is unnecessary.
    3. Now press A: check that your initials still appear, not B’s output.
    4. Press B again: check that its own response still works.

    Keep / change / explain: What stayed the same? What did you add? How does your new output help the person wearing or reading the badge?

    Language support

    You may draft in your own language and use the English prompts to help. Your teacher reviews your reasoning, not exact wording.
