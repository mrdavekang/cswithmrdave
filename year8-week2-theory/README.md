# Year 8 Term 1 Week 2 Theory

## Lesson

**Smart Badge Algorithm and Flowchart**

The app follows this learning spine:

`Smart Badge problem → decomposition → algorithm → flowchart`

It is designed for a 60-minute Year 8 lesson using these school stages:

1. Our mission — 78-word reading, reading confirmation and three short questions
2. Do Now — the existing sequence and input/output activities
3. Types of Learning — three evidence-based Knowledge, Skills and Understanding checks
4. Main Task 1A / 1B — algorithms, decomposition and flowchart practice
5. Main Task 2 — Create an A4 Smart Badge flowchart
6. Optional three-level extension
7. Learning Pitstop
8. Plenary
9. Review and PDF export

## Before Do Now

Allow approximately 3–4 minutes for the introduction. Students read why the welcome badge is useful, what it should do and what they will produce in this theory lesson. The reading distinguishes planning from writing code.

The three questions appear one at a time, only after the student confirms they have read the mission. Each question asks for one choice and gives an explanation after checking. An incorrect answer does **not** block progress: students may continue after feedback or revise and check again. No timer or perfect score is required. Students can reopen the reading without losing their answers.

Reading confirmation, question position, selected answers, checked attempts, corrections and timestamps are saved. The Review page, direct PDF, print report and JSON backup include the new responses and checked attempts.

Existing saved work is preserved. Students who had already attempted the previous version can continue from their saved position without being locked out by the new introduction; their new mission stage remains incomplete until they attempt it. Students with an unused saved entry begin with the reading. Teacher review bypasses the new restrictions and remains separate from student work.

## Types of Learning: evidence before a practice plan

Allow approximately 5–7 minutes. This replaces the previous learning-type buttons and strategy dropdown. Only one of the following cards is shown at a time:

- **Knowledge:** Yes/Not yet about explaining algorithm and decomposition. Yes opens two short explanation fields. Not yet (or a help request) opens an in-app glossary with the flowchart symbol key and a brief cover-and-recall task. Students confirm they tried recalling a meaning. Written explanations are saved for teacher review, not keyword-marked.
- **Skills:** Improve “Show something” to match the Button A / initials brief. Visible when/what/where prompts and an optional sentence frame support the attempt. Feedback supplies comparison criteria; students then self-check Yes/Not yet. Requesting help is a valid route even if they cannot yet write a sentence.
- **Understanding:** Interpret the arrow from Button A to DISPLAY initials in the existing worked flowchart. Incorrect choices or a help request reveal a guided explanation and a specific tracing target; they do not block continuation.
- **Practice plan:** The app derives concrete next steps from these attempts and help requests. The plan remains available in a compact expandable reminder in Main Tasks 1 and 2. There is no forced choice of one learning type, no pass score and no claim of verified mastery from a student's confidence rating.

All responses, checked attempts, self-checks, help requests, current card and derived practice plan are included in saving, backup and evidence reporting. Edits can be rechecked; changing a Skills sentence resets its self-check. Previous version choices are retained in reports when present, and existing completed stages are not relocked. English plus Mandarin, Korean and Bahasa Melayu support is available for every new card.

Teacher use: compare confidence with the explanations and practical evidence, not just completed ticks. At Main Task 1, ask students with a Knowledge target to explain the two terms. During the A4 task, check the when/what/where details and ask students to follow one arrow aloud. The plan is formative guidance, not a grading decision. If the extra checks need more time, protect the A4 task and plenary; use the optional extension flexibly.

## Main Task 1A / 1B: expanded practice

The five focused cards contain **14 core questions**, designed for about **18 minutes**, plus two optional deeper challenges. Each card has separate **Read & see an example** and **Try the questions** views. Switching views preserves all responses. This avoids putting the whole topic and every question into one long page.

| Card | Focus | Practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1A · Algorithms | Precise, ordered instructions; algorithm versus executable program | Identify a plan and evaluate precision (2 questions) |
| 1A · Decomposition | A whole badge problem split into welcome display, button input and personal response | Allocate two responsibilities and explain why decomposition helps debugging (3 questions) |
| 1B · Symbols | Large oval, parallelogram, rectangle and arrow outlines with distinct purpose labels | Explain the purpose of a flowchart; choose START and DISPLAY shapes; interpret arrows (4 questions) |
| 1B · Trace and repair | A clearly labelled, top-to-bottom badge flowchart | Predict the display before Button A and repair an arrow that skips input (2 questions) |
| 1B · Apply and test | Specific instructions and a testable expected outcome | Complete an exact icon instruction, rewrite the button response and propose an early-output test (3 questions) |

The optional challenges ask students to repair two different design faults and adapt the badge output for a visitor who cannot read initials. They are inside the last card and do not block Main Task 2 or replace the later optional extension.

There is **one check per small question group**, with feedback directly under each question. Multiple-choice items are checked; written explanations and test designs are saved for teacher review, not judged by keyword matching. Incorrect answers can continue after feedback. A specific guided-help request allows unfinished answers to be recorded honestly as needing support rather than as correct. No timer or perfect-score requirement is used.

All card responses, current view, checked attempts, revisions, help requests and attempted challenges are saved and included in review, JSON backup and PDF reporting. Existing responses and prior completion are preserved; a student who previously completed Main Task 1 is not relocked.

Suggested 60-minute pacing: introduction 3; Do Now 5; KSU checks 6; Main Task 1A/1B 18; A4 Main Task 2 18; Learning Pitstop 3; plenary 5; export 2. Use optional challenges within the practice time for early finishers, not as extra compulsory work. The timings are guidance, not enforced waiting periods.

For assessment, ask a student to explain why one job is separate from another, point to the slanted sides of an input/output shape, and justify their predicted test output. Completion only shows that the student attempted the work; use the written reasoning, support records and A4 work to judge understanding.

## Main Task 2: paper-first design

Two switchable cards, **Draw on A4** and **Explain & finish**, share a visible worked flowchart; the model is not hidden behind a reveal button. `smart_badge_paper_model.svg` shows a heart / Button A / JP example. `smart_badge_display_ideas.svg` shows 5×5 LED patterns and successive letters, not two initials squeezed into one matrix. Students adapt the icon and initials rather than copying.

The large teacher-check checkbox records a student's report that their teacher has seen the paper. It is not authenticated teacher sign-off. The mutually exclusive waiting option records **teacher review pending**. Neither requires a photo. A non-empty explanation plus either paper status (or optional image) allows continuation; there is no keyword or character-count marking. Teachers should judge the actual paper and explanation, not the completion badge. Teacher testing mode can continue without evidence.

English sentence frames and an example support the explanation. Mandarin, Korean and Bahasa Melayu guidance and a translated example appear openly when selected at entry. An optional own-language draft and the explanation are saved, backed up and included in the report. Translation is fixed support, not an automatic translation service.

## Button B extension

The extension now uses three switchable cards (about 12 minutes): **Choose**, **Draw**, and **Explain & test**. Only one card is shown at a time. Students can revisit any card or leave for the Learning Pitstop without completing the extension. Saving a non-empty response advances to the next card; there is no character-count or keyword pass mark. Teacher mode bypasses the writing requirement. Existing extension responses and completed work are preserved.

`assets/images/button_b_event_example.svg` is a labelled, simplified front-view instructional diagram, not a photograph or editor screenshot. It identifies B on the right and models a smile response. `assets/images/button_a_b_flowcharts.svg` shows two separate event-response plans side by side. The welcome image remains a startup action above the two plans; no arrow joins the end of A to the start of B. END means the response finishes, not that the device stops listening for button events. Students test B first, then A, then B again using their paper.

The distinction between an event and its response follows [Microsoft MakeCode's official button-event documentation](https://makecode.microbit.org/reference/input/on-button-pressed). The optional MakeCode Blocks walkthrough needs no hardware transfer. This remains a theory/design extension; random numbers, selection and radio are not required. Simultaneous button presses/event conflicts are outside this task.

The three original response fields remain saved and included in PDF/JSON exports. Mandarin, Korean and Bahasa Melayu guidance accompanies each card. Paper work needs no photograph. Teacher assessment: check purposeful B output, unchanged A output, correct symbols/arrows, independent event routes, and a reasoned paper test rather than judging completion alone.

## Evidence-based Learning Pitstop

Allow **8–10 minutes**, reducing the optional extension when needed. The old action dropdown is replaced by five short cards: Know, Write, Read, Reflect, Plan. This is a supported learning conversation, not an attainment test or personality classification.

- Knowledge: two recognition questions on algorithm/sequence and decomposition.
- Skills: one precise-instruction recognition question and a student's own instruction. The latter requires teacher review and is never keyword-marked.
- Understanding: three questions on a new Smart Badge greeting flowchart, distinguishing input, text processing and display output. The rectangle makes the greeting inside the program; the parallelogram displays it later. `pitstop_greeting_flowchart.svg` is the visible model.
- Each check offers “Not sure yet”, explains answers and records dated attempts/revisions. No minimum score or confidence tick is required. Three self-confidence ticks are reported separately from the six checked recognition questions. Revised answers must be rechecked before their scores are treated as current.
- Reflection: challenge level, novelty/progress, an app suggestion, a student-selected stage, and a written reason linking feelings to actual work. Students may disagree with the suggestion.
- Plan: one brief, adaptable sentence each for vocabulary retrieval, precise-instruction practice and flowchart tracing. Students may explain a specific need for help in their own language. Mandarin, Korean and Bahasa Melayu support appears with each card.

Suggestion rules are deliberately transparent: “too hard/stuck” suggests Drowning regardless of score; easy/no stretch plus all six correct and three confidence ticks suggests Treading water; unfamiliar learning without feeling overwhelmed suggests New learning; other practice/improvement suggests Consolidating. Easy-but-inconsistent evidence explicitly calls for discussion. These are planning heuristics, **not validated psychological or attainment ratings**. A student's confirmed stage and reason remain separate from the app's suggestion. Mixed feelings across K/S/U are valid. The aim is appropriate support and challenge, not constant struggle.

All answers, own writing, confidence, feelings, suggested/confirmed stage, plans and check histories appear in PDF/print review and JSON backups. Schema 7 preserves earlier completion and the old `pitstop_action` response where present. Teacher mode bypasses completion requirements. New student completion requires attempted checks and brief reflection/plans; wrong answers, requests for help and home-language writing can proceed.

The subject-specific plan/monitor/evaluate approach is informed by the [EEF guidance on metacognition and self-regulation](https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/metacognition-and-self-regulation). The school stage labels and app suggestion rules are not presented as EEF-validated categories. Teachers should discuss the work and feelings together, observe performance, and respond promptly to requests for help. A local static app does not notify the teacher automatically.
