What must you understand before answering?

A computer network connects computers or other devices using communication links so that they can communicate and exchange data. A network can let users share files, printers, software, messages, security services and backups. Connecting devices also creates costs and risks: equipment and maintenance are needed; unauthorised users may access data; malware can spread between connected devices; and a network failure can remove access to shared resources.

Networks can be classified in two different ways. LAN and WAN describe geographical scale: a LAN normally covers a small area such as one school site, while a WAN covers a wider area or connects separate sites. Wired and wireless describe how data travels. Wi-Fi therefore tells you the connection method, not whether the network is a LAN or WAN.

Core readingOxfordAQA specification 3.5 · Textbook Chapter 8, pp. 226–229

1 · What is a network?

A computer network connects computers or other devices through communication links so that data can be exchanged. Networks allow users to share information, printers, software, security services and backups. They also introduce costs and risks: equipment must be purchased and maintained; data may be accessed without permission; malware can spread; and a failed network device may remove access to shared resources.

2 · Scale is not method

A LAN usually covers a relatively small geographical area and is often controlled by one person or organisation. A WAN covers a wider area, often links several sites and may use connections with distributed ownership. The Internet is the largest WAN.

Wired and wireless describe how data travels, not the network’s geographical size. A school LAN can therefore contain both Ethernet cables and Wi-Fi access points.

3 · Medium depends on evidence

Copper twisted pair carries electrical signals. It is comparatively inexpensive and suitable for shorter connections, but electrical interference can affect it. Fibre optic carries pulses of light, supports longer high-bandwidth links and resists electrical interference, though installation can be more expensive or specialised. Wireless supports mobility, but coverage, obstruction, interference and security must be considered.
